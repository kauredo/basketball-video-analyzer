### Shipped 2026-09-08, second pass

app `#29`, website `#18` and `#19` (`#19` stacked on `#18`).

**Semantic colour had one hex doing two jobs** (app `#29`). A fill sits behind
white and needs 4.5:1 against white; the same idea drawn as text or a border
sits on the app's own grounds and needs 4.5:1 against those, which pulls the hex
the other way. Splitting fill from ink moved seven pairs, all measured against
`--bg-quaternary`, the lightest ground in the dark theme and the darkest in the
light one: white on danger 3.68 to 4.98, danger ink 3.90/3.68 to 4.53/4.95,
warning ink 4.52/1.63 to 5.63/4.89, info ink 3.12/2.37 to 4.86/5.38.

The audit read the dark theme only, so it never had the light-theme half, which
was the worse half. `#ff9800` text on light paper at 1.63:1 was the worst pair
in the app and appeared on no list.

Two more found while measuring. `.deletePresetBtn:hover` was declared twice in
`CategoryManager.module.css` and the later one won, painting `#f44336` on
`#ef5350`, about 1.3:1, so the delete icon vanished under the pointer. And the
danger buttons took `--text-primary`, which is `#212121` in the light theme, so
one rule painted white text in one theme and near-black in the other.

**The stats `.barTrack`** drew on `--bg-tertiary` at 1.14:1 against its card, so
a short bar had nothing to be short against. It carries a 1px `--track` outline
now, which is the same answer the scrub track already had under a different
name; `--scrub-track` is renamed `--track` because it states the extent of two
tracks.

**`container-type: inline-size` stays on `.clipLibrary`**, and this is a
rejection, not a deferral. Five `@container` breakpoints in that file depend on
it, because the clip grid reflows on the side panel's width rather than the
window's. Removing it would silently drop all five. `010`'s portal is the
correct workaround, not a workaround for a mistake. The reason now lives at the
declaration instead of only in `PresentMode`.

**The 3D scene was `fixed inset-0`** (website `#18`), so it was a layer over the
whole document rather than the hero's backdrop, and every section below had to
paint an opaque ground. It is `absolute inset-0` inside the hero now, 900px
against a 5493px document, and the component is `HeroBasketball3D` rather than
`FullScreenBasketball3D`, because a name that says full screen is how `fixed`
comes back. The standing rule about painting grounds is gone with it.

**The ruled bands** (website `#18`). Divisions were a ground change of 1.05:1
and the two `warm-100` sections had no division at all. Every band edge carries
a 1px `warm-300` rule now, which is the direction's `#DED5C4` within a shade and
the rule Coaching guides had been using alone. Two edges have none on purpose:
More features continues Present mode rather than dividing from it, and the hero
keeps its rule because below `sm` the scene never loads and the rule is then the
only thing marking where the hero ends.

**`DESIGN.md` described a proposal, not the product** (website `#19`). Of the
seventeen neutrals it specified across both surfaces, none shipped; only the
accent matched, exactly. The 2026-09-07 rewrite had been meant to fix this and
reproduced it, and the 2026-09-08 drift note caught only the site half, so the
app table spent another day specifying a plum-cast graphite that exists in no
file. The tables read from source now, and what was aspirational sits under
"Chosen but not built" with the cost of adopting it. That includes the
direction's "no cards, no shadows, no radii above 1px", which the app
contradicts 265 times and which is a decision rather than a migration.

The lesson, and it is the same one twice: **a design document that claims to
record the product will drift, and the drift is invisible because the document

## The locale pass, 2026-09-08

App `#32`. Asked for as a style check on the ten translations. The style
problems were in the English, and every locale had faithfully translated them:
three em dashes, nine uses of "successfully", five exclamation marks, and two
hyphens joining clauses that every translator upgraded to a dash. The same
padding sat in all ten files in ten different words. 137 strings changed, and
there is no longer an exclamation mark anywhere in the app.

**Everything that mattered was found underneath the style work, not by it.**

- **Nine locales had no `app.startup` block at all.** Those three strings are
  what the app shows when its database will not open. Every non-English user
  hit English at the one moment the app is already broken.
- **The onboarding named keys that do nothing.** `app.welcome.steps.markClips`
  said "Use I and O keys" in English and in all ten translations. The defaults
  are `markInKey: "z"` and `markOutKey: "m"`, and two other English strings say
  Z and M correctly. This survived plan `015`, which was specifically "fix
  wrong shortcut keys".
- **`pt` and `tr` translated the product name**, and in Portuguese it wrapped
  the header onto two lines and pushed into the video pane. Plan `016` had
  already asked for one product name everywhere.
- **`pt` was missing the seven default category names**, so a Portuguese
  project seeded in English.
- **`pt` spoke two registers**, 26 strings of você against 2 of tu. The
  operator chose tu, so 52 strings converted the other way.

Three lessons, and they are all the same one.

**An audit that reads files cannot tell which way round a mismatch goes.** The
audit listed `app.title` as "untranslated" in eight locales and flagged it.
The eight were right and the two translated ones were wrong. Rendering the app
in Portuguese showed that in one frame.

**The i18n checks worth running are structural, not linguistic.** Key parity,
placeholder integrity and register consistency found real bugs in languages
nobody here reads. "Needs a native speaker" was true of about a dozen strings
and false of everything above.

**Removing a word is not translating.** Stripping "successfully" from ten
languages needed no fluency, which is why it was safe to do at all.

## 026: the mark, 2026-09-08

### The geometry, for anyone who touches the mark again

Established twice, independently, from a patent and from photographs:

- Two great circles meeting at 90 degrees at the poles, plus two closed cheek
  loops. Six crossings, twelve edges, eight panels.
- **The cheek seam is not a wave.** Constant longitude for most of its length,
  then a hard turn about 15 degrees from the pole. Measured at 15.5, 14.9, 15.2
  and 16.9 degrees across four arcs in two photographs. As a series it needs a
  `cos4psi` term; a pure cosine draws a volleyball, which is exactly what the
  first correct-topology attempt produced.
- **Face-on shows three seams, not four.** The fourth great circle projects onto
  the silhouette. The familiar four-line icon is a pole-on view at roughly double
  the real amplitude, i.e. a stylisation. Never draw the ball face-on.
- **Seams meet the silhouette tangentially**, 73 degrees rising to 90, except a
  great circle seen edge-on, which meets near-perpendicular at 21 to 23 degrees
  from radial.
- A real seam is 2.2% of ball diameter. The mark uses 3.79%, which is a logo
  decision, not an accident.

Redraw fidelity: within 0.0288 R of the traced samples at worst, 0.0068 rms,
against points it was never fitted to. The waist profile, also never fitted,
lands within 0.016 of the measured one out to |u| = 0.6.

**The 16px cut is a separate drawing and must stay one.** Four seam strands do
not fit in sixteen pixels; three passes concluded that independently. The
favicon drops the upper cheek and thickens the rest. Thinning instead gives mush.

Generator, traces, measurements and every rejected candidate are in the session
scratchpad under `marks/`. `Q-ball-q2.svg` is what shipped.

### A merge mistake worth not repeating

The app stack was merged with `gh pr merge --merge --delete-branch`, bottom-up.
**Do not pass `--delete-branch` when merging a stack.** Deleting each base
branch broke the chain before GitHub retargeted the next PR: `#12` and `#14`
closed unmerged, and `#13` and `#15` merged into intermediate branches instead
of the integration branch. Only plan 019 reached `feat/scorebook-identity` on
the first pass.

No work was lost, because every commit stayed reachable from the surviving
branches, but the per-plan PR split did not survive. The remainder went in as
one recovery PR, `#16`, which supersedes `#12` through `#15`.

The website stack was then merged correctly, and this is the pattern to use:
merge the bottom PR, then **explicitly `gh pr edit <n> --base <integration
branch>`** on the next one before merging it, and never delete branches until
the whole stack is in. Website `#6`, `#7`, `#8` all landed as proper merges that
way.

Three CSS facts that cost attempts and should not be rediscovered:

  `nowrap` clips controls off the right edge at 1180px and below, which is worse
  than wrapping.

## 023 shipped, 2026-09-07 (app PR #25)

Stacked on the batch D chain: `#21` -> `#22` -> `#23` -> `#24` -> `#25`.

**The measured problem.** Twelve of the 105 default colour pairs sat under CIE76
dE 22, worst Defense against Man-to-Man at 9.9 and Rebounding against its own
child Offensive Rebound at 11.0. The audit's threshold of 22 refers to **CIE76**,
not CIEDE2000; under CIEDE2000 the same palette has 21 failing pairs. Use CIE76
when re-checking.

**The new palette** gives every category its own hue: minimum pairwise dE 23.7,
every colour clearing 3:1 on both theme grounds and sitting 25 or more from the
action and danger colours.

### Colour alone cannot carry fifteen categories, and this is why

Requiring one hex to clear 3:1 on **both** `#2a2a2a` and `#f5f5f5` pins its
luminance to roughly [0.17, 0.27], and lightness is most of what a deuteranope
discriminates with. Measured maximum palette size at dE 22:

| Swatch contrast | Normal vision | Also colourblind-safe |
|---|---|---|
| >= 3:1 both themes | 16 | **7** |
| >= 2.5:1 both themes | 16 | 12 |
| unconstrained | 16 | 16 |

The preset has fifteen. The operator chose to keep 3:1 and add the second
signal, so the timeline's per-lane shape is the guarantee rather than a nicety.
Do not treat Step 3 as optional in any future revision.

### Two things the plan did not mention, both of which would have shipped broken

- **The preset seed is `INSERT OR IGNORE` behind an early return**, so an
  existing install would never have seen a palette change, not even on a new
  project. The migration has to sit **above** that return. It re-colours only
  rows still holding the old default, so a preset the user recoloured keeps
  their value and per-project `categories` rows are untouched.
- **`DEFAULT_CATEGORY_COLOR` was still `#4CAF50`**, which lands dE 7.4 from the
  new Transition and fails light-theme contrast at 2.55:1, so "no colour set"
  would have looked like a real category.

Also: `box-shadow` does not accumulate across rules, so a hollow marker drawn
with an inset shadow lost its ring to `.selectedClip`'s shadow at higher
specificity. Use `outline` with a negative offset for a ring that has to
coexist with a shadow.

A correctness review confirmed the migration's call order, idempotency and that
it cannot reach user data. Its only finding was the box-shadow collision, which
had already been fixed; it had read the rule before that commit landed.
